# Building a course's lessons — partner guide

For course editors in the SEQ Elevate consortium. It covers the lesson editor that has been live since 13 October, and what changes from 10 November: learners get courses exactly as you build them, and a course people are taking can still be changed, with a few rules that protect their work. Everything here happens on a course's own page: **Manage courses**, then open the course.

## In one minute

- Each course you create has a **lesson list** (the two courses that came with the platform keep their fixed layout). You can add lessons, give them your own names in each language, move them, hide them and delete them.
- Inside each lesson you can **add content** around the lesson's main content: text, a practice question, a branching scenario, a quick check, reflection questions, or a 1–5 self-assessment.
- **Preview as learner** shows what learners get, at any time.
- From 10 November, **learners get the course as you built it**: your own lessons and names, your order, the content you added. Hidden lessons are left out. A course publishes as long as learners would see at least one lesson.
- **Once people are taking a course**, adding, moving, hiding or showing lessons, and adding, moving or removing content, first tell you how many learners it affects and ask you to confirm. On a published course with learners, no lesson can be deleted: hide it instead. On a course that isn't published, only a lesson no learner has anything in can be deleted, until 1 December. A question learners may have answered can't be removed while they are there; you can still edit it.
- **From 1 December**, a course with learners keeps its shape, published or not: lessons and content can no longer be added, moved or removed. Hiding and showing lessons, renaming, and editing text and questions stay open.
- Nothing you wrote before 13 October moves or changes. Existing courses look and work as they did.
- **From 20 November, German and Greek are switched on per course.** Write the course in English first; learners using German or Greek get it in English until their language is switched on (see *Switching German or Greek on*).
- From 26 November you can **link words** in your text with the **Link** button (Ctrl+K, ⌘K on Mac), and links in text you paste from Word or Google Docs are kept.
- Each lesson can list **further reading** next to its documents, and each course a **References & bibliography** list.

## The lesson list

The list is on the left of the course page (above the lessons on a phone). Each lesson has an **Actions** button (…) with:

| Action | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Rename** | A name of your own, per language, up to 40 characters. Switch the content language at the top of the page to name it in German or Greek. Learners see it at once. |
| **Move up / Move down** | Changes the lesson's place in the course. |
| **Hide / Show** | Keeps a lesson in the course but out of what learners get. |
| **Delete** | Removes the lesson in all languages, with everything in it. The page lists what goes first, and asks you to confirm. |

**Add lesson** (below the list) offers: Blank, Text, Practice question, Branching scenario, Quick check, or Reflection & self-assessment. Choose where it goes, then press **Add this lesson**. A standard lesson you deleted can be added back, empty.

Limits: up to **15 lessons** per course, hidden ones included. Past **12 lessons learners see**, the page asks you to check that learners need them all.

A lesson of your own that has nothing in it yet is marked "Learners get this lesson as soon as it has content": it appears to learners once it has something they can get — text you saved, a video, a published document, or a question saved with **Save stage** (a draft alone doesn't count). Further-reading links never make a lesson appear: a lesson with nothing but links stays out of what learners get.

## Content in a lesson

Open a lesson to edit it. Its content is a numbered list: the lesson's **main content** (the text or exercise it always had) and anything you add.

- **Add content** sits after each piece of content. Choose the kind; it appears there, ready to write.
- **Move up / Move down** and **Remove** sit on each added piece. Removing asks first and removes it in all languages. The main content can't be removed this way.
- A lesson holds at most 12 pieces of content, the main one included.

Questions and exercises save as they always did:
- **Save draft** keeps unfinished work for editors only.
- **Save stage** checks that the content is complete and makes it ready. Learners get a question once it has been saved this way; until then they don't see it.
- **Discard** goes back to the last saved version.

Added text saves per language. If a colleague saved the same content while you were editing, your save is refused and you can load their version. Nothing is overwritten unseen.

**Reflection questions** replace the lesson's standard reflection prompts with your own. **Self-assessment** asks learners to rate statements from 1 to 5. You write the two ends of the scale.

## Links in your text

- Select a few words, press **Link** (or Ctrl+K / ⌘K), type the address, press Enter or **Apply**. If you click away without pressing Apply, the link isn't added. **Edit link** and **Remove link** work the same way. Put the cursor in a link to see where it goes ("Link: …" under the toolbar). Link words that say where the link goes, not "click here".
- Addresses must start with `https://` or `http://`, or be an email address. `http://` works but the editor warns: use `https://` if the site offers it.
- Links open in a new tab for learners, email links included (they open the learner's email program). Use a team or role address, not a person's own address.
- Learners see the site each link opens in a small box after its words, for example "the guide [example.org]", so they always know where a link goes.
- A link's words may name a site only if it is the one the link opens: "bpb.de" linked to www.bpb.de is fine, but "paypal.com" linked to another site is refused, by the editor at once and on Save.
- Typed addresses still become links on their own, as since 13 October.
- Pasting from Word or Google Docs keeps web and email links; links to files on your computer, Word's bookmarks and footnote links become plain text, and the editor says how many links weren't kept and why.
- Outlook "safe links" (addresses starting with something like `https://eur01.safelinks.protection.outlook.com/`) carry your mailbox address. Pasted, or typed into the Link form, they are turned back into the original address; if that address can't be used, the link isn't kept. Best: copy the address from the website itself.
- To share a file, upload it as a document; links to files uploaded to the platform are refused.
- Check that every link opens in a private browser window: SharePoint, OneDrive and Teams links often work only for you.
- Links work in paragraphs, key messages and the reflection introduction. A **List** block (Bulleted or Numbered list from "Add a block") and comparisons and exercise texts keep typed addresses only. To link words in a list, make the list with the list buttons inside a **Paragraph** block instead.
- Before you save, check each link: put the cursor in it, or open **Preview as learner**.
- A link you add to a published course reaches learners when you save, like any text.

## Further reading

**Add a link** sits next to **Upload document** in each lesson: a title, the web address, a short description (optional) and the languages it is for. New links are **drafts** until you choose **Show to learners**. Up to 30 per lesson. The title must not show a different address than the link opens. Learners see them under "Further reading" at the end of the lesson: before **Continue** in a lesson of your own or a lesson you added content to, and after **Continue** in a standard lesson with only its main content (as in every lesson of the two courses that came with the platform). If you wrote a "Further reading" list at the end of a lesson's text (as we advised before 26 November), move it into links, so learners don't see it twice.

## A language for each document or link

Tick the languages a document or link is for (all three by default). Learners get the items for every language and the items for their language. Until German or Greek is switched on for a course, German or Greek learners get the course text in English, so they also get the English items, and their own language's items marked "In German" or "In Greek"; **Preview as learner** in German shows the German text and the German items. Languages never change which lessons learners get or how progress counts: a lesson whose only material is a German document still reaches English and Greek learners, who then see that document, marked "In German" (the course page says so).

## References & bibliography

On the course page, below the title and subtitle (courses you created). One entry per paragraph, in APA 7 style, for example: "Author, A. A. (Year). *Title of the work*. Publisher. https://doi.org/…" Use italics for titles and the Link button for addresses. Write it in English first; a German or Greek list replaces it for those learners only when you write one. It goes live when you save. Learners open it from the top of the course (in a new tab) and from the completion screen; each entry is set with a hanging indent. At most 10,000 characters per language. Unsaved changes are kept if you switch language: the page asks first.

## Preview as learner

**Preview as learner** (top of the course page) opens the course as a learner gets it: your lessons in your order, with your names and content, hidden lessons left out.

- **Edit this lesson** takes you back to the lesson you are looking at.
- Nothing you do in a preview is saved: no progress, no statistics, no badge.

## Publishing

From 10 November a course you created can be published with lessons of your own, hidden or deleted standard lessons, a new order and added content. **Publish** refuses only when learners couldn't take the course:

- **no lesson has anything learners can see** (every lesson is hidden or empty), or
- **something can't be read** (the lesson list, or a lesson's content). The page names the lesson. Contact the platform team, who can repair it.
- **the English isn't complete**, the first time a course you created is published (from 20 November). English is the source language: the page lists what has no English yet. On a course already published, the same thing is a warning and nothing is unpublished.

On a published course, hiding or deleting the last lesson learners can see is refused. Removing a lesson's last content isn't: that lesson then leaves what learners get (until it has content again), and if it was the last one, learners find a course with no lessons yet. Check with **Preview as learner**.

## Switching German or Greek on

From 20 November, a course you created reaches learners in German or Greek only once that language is **switched on** for it. Until then, learners using German or Greek get the whole course in English, with a note saying so, rather than a mix of languages. The two courses that came with the platform are in every language already and have no switch.

1. **Write the course in English first.** English is the source language: a course is first published only when its English is complete, and English is what learners get wherever a translation is missing.
2. **Translate it.** Switch the content language at the top of the course page to German or Greek, and write each lesson's text and exercises in it. **Preview as learner** in that language shows the translation as learners will get it, with a banner while the language isn't switched on yet.
3. **Have it signed off and switched on.** Under **Publish**, the panel **Languages for learners** has one switch per language, with how much of the course is written in it. German is signed off by Pro Arbeit's partner admin, Greek by the partner admin of a Greek-speaking partner (Synthesis or the University of Macedonia), whoever built the course. Until partner admins are invited (25 November), email DIESIS or SENIC to switch a language on; they do it at your written request, and the platform records who did.

What the switch's coverage checks, and what it doesn't:

- It counts the **main text and exercises** of each lesson. Switching on a language that isn't complete asks first; learners then see English wherever text is missing.
- It does **not** check added content, lesson names, video questions or documents. Check those yourself in **Preview as learner** before you switch the language on. Coverage doesn't count documents, further reading or references: check them yourself in Preview as learner.
- If the English changes after a language is live, the panel warns that some text is now missing in that language. The language stays on; translate the new text, and learners get English there until you do.

Editors who can't switch a language see the panel read-only. On **Manage courses**, a language that is live for learners says "live" beside its name.

## Courses that have learners

A learner is someone who has started the course, i.e. saved an answer or finished a lesson. Opening a course doesn't make anyone a learner, and editors or staff trying a course out never count.

### Changes that ask first

On a course with learners, these changes first open a short panel where you asked for them: how many learners are taking the course, how many finished, and what they will notice. Press the change's own button (**Move it**, **Hide it**, **Show it**, **Add it**, **Remove it**) to make it, or **Cancel** (or Escape) to leave everything as it was.

| Change | What learners notice |
|---|---|
| Move a lesson | Their progress is kept. Those who haven't finished see the new order next time and carry on from the first lesson they haven't done. |
| Hide a lesson | It leaves their course. What they did in it is kept, still counts, and comes back if you show it again. |
| Add a lesson, or show one again | Learners who haven't finished have one more lesson to do. Those who finished keep their badge and stay at 100%. |
| Add, move or remove content in a lesson (text, or a question learners don't get yet) | Learners who have done the lesson keep it as done; others see the change when they reach it. |
| Remove a lesson's only content (all learners get of it) | The lesson leaves their course until it has content again. What they did in it is kept and still counts. |

The platform checks the confirmation when you save. If the course got its first learner while your page was open, you are asked then, with the count it found. Every confirmed change is recorded with the number of learners.

### Deleting, and removing questions

- **Deleting a lesson** on a published course with learners is refused: the page says how many learners have started and offers **Hide it** instead, which keeps their work. On a course that isn't published, a lesson can be deleted as long as no learner has anything in it, until 1 December (below).
- **A question learners may have answered** (one saved with *Save stage*) can't be removed while the course has learners. You can still edit it.
- **Remove stage** on a lesson's exercise learners get is refused while the course has learners, whether it is a standard lesson or one of your own.

### From 1 December

From 1 December, a course with learners keeps its shape, published or not: lessons and content can no longer be added, moved or removed, and no lesson can be deleted. Unpublishing a course to rework it and publishing it again doesn't change that. The buttons stay visible and say so, with the date. You can still:

- hide and show lessons (to take one out, use **Hide**);
- rename lessons;
- edit text and questions, video and documents (live at once, as always).

A course nobody has started yet can be restructured as before.

## Questions partners have asked

- **Will my existing course change?** No. Its text, exercises, drafts, video and documents stay exactly where they are. The list shows the same lessons in the same order.
- **Can I publish my course with a lesson of my own?** Yes, from 10 November. Use Preview as learner to check it first.
- **I need to restructure a course after 1 December.** Hide what learners shouldn't see. For anything more, talk to the platform team.
- **Can two of us edit at once?** Yes. If you both change the same thing, the second save is refused with a message, and the page shows the other person's version.
- **Translations:** each lesson name and text is per language. Until German or Greek is switched on for a course, its learners get it in English; once it is on, they get English wherever a translation is missing.

*German and Greek wording on screen is a first draft and is being reviewed by partners.*
